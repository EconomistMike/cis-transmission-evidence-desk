# Hunter Transmission Project

## Overview

HTP-PROJECT-HTP | Research cut-off: 2026-09-04 | 8 sources | 39 observations

## Identity and status

**Identity.** One provisional Project, also named Sydney Ring North (AEMO CNSW-SNW Option 1). AEMO's 2026 representation bundles the Waratah Super Battery and related upgrades; WSB is treated as provisionally distinct because its own lifecycle records were not captured.

**Current status.** Actionable New South Wales project under the Electricity Infrastructure Investment Act 2020 (NSW); AEMO reports the project is in the NSW Government's independent assessment phase, with construction expected to start in late 2026 if approved. No EnergyCo approval, business case or statutory authorisation record was captured.

**Capacity.** AEMO 2026 reports forward additional network capacity of 5,000 MW (REZ N10: 2,000 MW); AEMO 2024 reported the same up-to-5,000 MW observation. This is transfer-capacity/hosting wording, not a rating of the single line.

**Schedule.** Timing advised by proponent: November 2029 (2026 ISP); December 2028 (2024 ISP).

**Owner.** EnergyCo NSW is the Infrastructure Planner for the project as a priority transmission infrastructure project; no EnergyCo delivery record was captured.

**Funding pathway.** Priority transmission infrastructure project under the Electricity Infrastructure Investment Act 2020 (NSW); progresses as an actionable New South Wales project rather than under the RIT-T/ISP framework.

**Delivery status.** Pre-construction; NSW Government independent assessment phase per AEMO 2026.

**Dependent projects.** AEMO makes CNSW-SNW Option 1 (HTP) a pre-requisite for the Central-West Orana REZ network project above 3 GW and for the Sydney Ring South power flow control augmentation (CNSW-SNW Option 2d).

## Timeline

- 2022-06 — Northern Sydney Ring named the Hunter Transmission Project (Actionable New South Wales project): AEMO's 2022 ISP records that the northern part of the Sydney Ring is named the Hunter Transmission Project and may include the Waratah Super Battery and related upgrades, progressing under the Electricity Infrastructure Investment Act 2020 (NSW). [HUM-AEMO-2022-ISP; 70]
- 2022-12-09 — Hunter-Central Coast REZ declared (Related REZ declaration): NSW Government Gazette No 569 published the Renewable Energy Zone (Hunter-Central Coast) Order 2022, declaring the HCC REZ, its specified geographical area and network infrastructure, with an intended network capacity of one gigawatt. This is the REZ within which HTP sits; the gazette does not declare, authorise or name HTP itself. [HTP-NSWGOV-2022-GAZETTE-569; 7]
- 2023-05 — NSW Network Infrastructure Strategy published (Strategy / Deliver Now project): EnergyCo scopes HTP Stage 1 as a 500 kV double circuit from Bayswater to Eraring providing 5 GW of downstream capacity, scheduled for mid-2027 at approximately $1 billion, with a possible Stage 2 after 2033 adding 6 GW at up to $1.8 billion. [CWO-04; 46-47]
- 2024-06 — 2024 ISP representation (Actionable NSW project): AEMO reports Option 1 at $1,099 million (±50%), 5,000 MW additional network capacity, timing advised by proponent December 2028, and notes HTP should be considered when Central West Orana REZ exceeds 3 GW. [HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5; 23-24]
- 2026-06 — 2026 ISP representation (Actionable New South Wales project; NSW independent assessment phase): AEMO reports CNSW-SNW Option 1 at $1,364 million Class 5b (±50%), forward 5,000 MW (REZ N10: 2,000 MW), timing advised by proponent November 2029, and states construction is expected to start in late 2026 if the project is approved. [HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5; 38-39]
- 2025-12 — AER final decision on Ausgrid's Hunter-Central Coast REZ non-contestable revenue (Related REZ regulatory decision): AER determined Ausgrid can recover $189.6 million ($ nominal) for 2026-31 for the Hunter-Central Coast REZ non-contestable network infrastructure project, covering distribution-level works at Muswellbrook, Singleton to Kurri, and a new Antiene switching station. This is a related REZ project's revenue determination, not an HTP cost; neither the Hunter Transmission Project nor HTP is named anywhere in this decision. [HTP-AER-2025-FINALDECISION; 7, 9]
- 2026-09-03 — EnergyCo, NSW planning and WSB acquisition attempts failed (Unavailable): Six publication targets were attempted over two distinct official routes each; EnergyCo project and infrastructure pages returned HTTP 403 and the assessment, planning/EIS, authorisation and WSB/SIPS targets returned HTTP 404. No project-local capture exists.

## Warnings and open decisions

- AUTHOR_DECISION_REQUIRED#11 (warning): alt_routes = 1 is an author definition: completing the northern Sydney Ring gives an alternative 500 kV path south from Bayswater, discounted where the route parallels the existing 330 kV corridor. No archived source counts alternative routes, and the NSW Network Infrastructure Strategy's third-corridor indication for HTP Stage 2 appears only on a map, not in the archived text derivative. The author must confirm the counting rule and accept that the cell is a judgment, not a sourced value. [CWO-04; CWO-04 pp46-47]
- AUTHOR_DECISION_REQUIRED#12 (warning): syncons_mva = 0, hubs = 0 and feeder_lines_km = 0 are author inferences from scope lists that do not state absence; the Transgrid system strength PACR and the EIS the author cites were not captured. The author must decide whether to publish zeros as asserted absences or leave the cells blank; they are recorded here as gaps.
- NON_COMPARABLE_SERIES#1 (warning): The $1,364m (2026 ISP), $1,099m (2024 ISP) and approximately $1 billion (NSW Network Infrastructure Strategy, May 2023, HTP Stage 1) amounts are separate dated observations with different vintages, price bases and scope definitions. The 2023 figure is in real 2021 dollars and excludes finance, generator connection and system strength costs; neither ISP table states a dollar year. The $265m difference between the two ISP amounts is not an overrun, a revision or a cost series. Do not chart these three figures as a trend or compute an escalation. [HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5; CWO-04; HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5 p39; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5 p24; CWO-04 pp46-47]
- PRICE_BASIS_UNKNOWN#2 (warning): Neither the 2026 nor the 2024 ISP Appendix A5 option table states the dollar year for the reported HTP cost. The chart's $1.36bn cannot be placed on a stated price basis, and cannot be deflated or compared with the EnergyCo real 2021 dollar estimate. [HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5; HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5 p39; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5 p24]
- NON_ADDITIVE_BOUNDARIES#3 (warning): AEMO's 2026 HTP representation states the project 'includes the WSB and related upgrades', while the 2024 representation said it 'may include' them; no WSB cost, capacity or scope record was captured. The reported cost sits on a bundled boundary that cannot be decomposed from archived sources. Do not add WSB amounts to HTP, and do not treat the reported cost as covering only the Olney-Bayswater South works. [HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5; HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5 p38; HUM-AEMO-2024-ISP-A5 p23]
- SHARED_ATTRIBUTION#4 (warning): The 5,000 MW figure is a network hosting/transfer uplift enabling exports from the Central-West Orana, New England and Hunter-Central Coast REZs; the EnergyCo strategy frames the same project as lifting combined supportable REZ output from 3 GW to as much as 8 GW. The chart's 5 GW exceeds the transfer capacity of the single double-circuit line and is shared with other projects' capacity claims. Do not treat it as an asset rating or compute a cost per MW. [HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5; CWO-04; HUM-AEMO-2026-ISP-A5 pp38-39; CWO-04 p46]
- SHARED_ATTRIBUTION#5 (warning): The AER's December 2025 final decision determines $189.6 million ($ nominal) total revenue for 2026-31 for Ausgrid's Hunter-Central Coast REZ non-contestable network infrastructure project (distribution-level works at Muswellbrook, Singleton to Kurri, and a new Antiene switching station), and NSW Government Gazette No 569 (9 December 2022) declares the Hunter-Central Coast REZ with an intended network capacity of one gigawatt. Neither document mentions the Hunter Transmission Project or 'HTP' anywhere. The $189.6 million revenue figure and the 1 GW REZ capacity are Ausgrid distribution-level amounts for a separate, related REZ project; they must never be added to, compared with, or substituted for HTP's own cost observations (HTP-AO-2026-COST, HTP-AO-2024-COST, HTP-AO-2023-NIS-STAGE1-COST) or capacity observations (HTP-AO-2026-CAPACITY-FORWARD and related). HTP and the HCC Project both sit within the Hunter-Central Coast REZ and share that geography, but are delivered by different Network Operators under different statutory processes. [HTP-AER-2025-FINALDECISION; HTP-NSWGOV-2022-GAZETTE-569; HTP-AER-2025-FINALDECISION pp7,9; HTP-NSWGOV-2022-GAZETTE-569 p7]
